Gastronomy

Breakfast is the honest meal

By Noor Haddad · 24 February 2026 · 5 min read

Dinner is a performance. The lighting is arranged, the kitchen is at full strength, and the whole apparatus is pointed at making one evening memorable. It is not a reliable guide to anything.

Breakfast is where a hotel tells the truth, because nobody is trying. It is served at the least glamorous hour by the smallest team to guests who have not yet put their faces on, and every economy the property has made is visible on the plate.

The buffet problem

A vast buffet reads as generosity and functions as the opposite. It is the cheapest way to feed a full house, it guarantees that nothing has been cooked for you, and it converts the quietest hour of the day into a queue. The eggs have been warm for forty minutes. Everyone knows this and the format survives anyway.

A short breakfast menu cooked to order costs the property more and the guest less. Almost nobody does it.

The properties worth returning to have generally abandoned it: a page with six things on it, all made when asked, plus bread, fruit and something local that you did not choose. It is faster, it is quieter, and it is the single clearest signal that the kitchen is run by someone who eats there.

What to look for

  • Is the bread from here? The most reliable single indicator in hospitality. Good local bread means relationships with the town; industrial rolls mean a delivery contract.
  • Is there one thing you would not get at home? A regional cheese, a preserved fruit, a dish the region actually eats in the morning. Its presence means somebody chose it.
  • Can you have it late? A kitchen that stops at half past nine is running to a schedule that is not yours.
  • Is the coffee made by a person? Not a machine in the corner. This one is nearly infallible.

And the room service version

Frequently the best option, and rarely the one taken. Breakfast on a terrace, at your own hour, in whatever you slept in, is one of the genuine pleasures of staying somewhere good — and on a slow itinerary there is no reason at all to be downstairs at eight.

Judge a place by its breakfast. It is the meal it cannot dress up.
